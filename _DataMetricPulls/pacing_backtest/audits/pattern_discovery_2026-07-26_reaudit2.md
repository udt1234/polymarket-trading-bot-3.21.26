### [CLASS D] FINDING 1 (this round, MEDIUM-HIGH) -- the flipped PRIMARY flag is a mathematically near-degenerate n=3 CI, not new evidence, and the disclosure's own claim that "nothing changed" is FALSE

What changed, confirmed by full-column diff, not by reading the change-log: compared every row of the current per_row.csv (225,039 rows) against _prereaudit_backup_2026-07-29/per_row.csv (the WARN-verdict run) on the join key (target, auction_slug, checkpoint_hours, bracket, method, span). Result: market_price, is_winner, price_coverage_n/tot, b4_admissible are 0/225,039 different (byte-identical). model_prob differs on exactly 35,029 rows, and 100% of those rows belong to methods M5 (7,337), M6 (17,155), M7(M6) (10,537) -- the three methods that consume RNG_M5/RNG_M6. Every deterministic method (B1, B2, B3, B4, M1, M2, M3, M4, M7(M1)) is exact-string-identical across the two runs, 166,721/166,721 rows. This closes question 1 definitively: the flip is attributable to the RNG re-seed alone. No other logic, data, or gate changed.

Where the flip actually lives: the WARN run's own summary.md (preserved in the backup) reports M7(M6) vs B4, elon_7day sealed: skill=+0.2711 CI[-0.0056,+0.6654] n=3 -> unproven (CI incl. 0). This run reports skill=+0.2597 CI[+0.0032,+0.7573] n=3 -> BEATS. The mean barely moved (+0.2711 -> +0.2597, ~4% relative), but the verdict flipped entirely because the lower CI bound crossed zero by 0.0088 (-0.0056 -> +0.0032) -- a shift smaller than typical Monte-Carlo sampling noise from a 150-path Ogata-thinning simulator.

Why the CI is close to meaningless at this n, independently verified: the 3 admissible auctions and their per-auction (B4_logloss - M7(M6)_logloss) skill values are:
- elon-musk-of-tweets-may-29-june-5:   +0.01865
- elon-musk-of-tweets-june-2-june-9:   +0.00321
- elon-musk-of-tweets-june-5-june-12:  +0.75732

block_bootstrap_ci with block_size=1 on n=3 can only ever produce a mean that is a convex combination (weighted average) of these 3 fixed numbers -- enumerated all 3^3=27 possible resamples exhaustively (not just the 2000 Monte-Carlo draws the script uses) and confirmed the full support is bounded exactly by [min, max] = [+0.0032, +0.7573], with 0 of the 27 possible resamples ever negative, because all 3 raw values already share the same sign. This means "CI excludes zero" at n=3/block_size=1 is mathematically equivalent to "do all 3 raw auctions happen to have the same sign," nothing more -- it carries none of the statistical-power meaning a reader expects from a 95% CI. Under a null of zero true skill with independent coin-flip signs, P(all 3 same sign) = 25%, five times the nominal 5% false-positive rate the CI notation implies. Two of the three margins (0.0032 and 0.0186 nats) are themselves an order of magnitude smaller than the sampling noise expected from a 150-path Monte Carlo simulator (illustratively, sqrt(p(1-p)/150) ~ 0.03-0.04 on a bracket probability, which routinely moves per-checkpoint log loss by more than these two "wins" in total). The entire mean is carried by ONE auction (june-5-june-12, contributing +0.757 of the +0.260 average); jackknife-drop-best (removing exactly that auction) leaves a mean of only +0.0109 -- a razor's edge that would not survive a third reseed with any confidence.

The pre-registration's PRIMARY criterion is "CI excludes zero," full stop, with no stated n-floor for when that test is even meaningful, even though the pre-registration's own scoring section separately states CI-including-zero means unproven and Pass D's standard (n<10 -> noise) is well understood in this project.
