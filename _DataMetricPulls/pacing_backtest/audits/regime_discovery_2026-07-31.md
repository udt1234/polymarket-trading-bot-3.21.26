## THE ONE POSITIVE FINDING -- attacked directly (hypothesis c, "HOLDS IN REVERSE")

1. Sealed n behind the CI, and degeneracy check. Formal test: n_fired=13 sealed days (3 positive at +0.368, 10 negative at -0.632 -- mixed sign, not the all-one-sign tiny-n degenerate case from lesson_tiny_n_ci_degenerate.md). n_fired=13 clears SUCCESS_N_FLOOR=10. Literal test's SEALED corroboration slice is n_big_morning=8, below the floor (see findings below). Formal train evidence n=57 fired / 212 total.
2. Lag-1-persistence-in-disguise check. The report's own prose already concedes the mechanistic point unprompted: "mostly just re-measures that early activity correlates with total activity ... not genuine within-day exhaustion ... mild momentum/day-level heterogeneity (some days are just busier throughout)." The auditor additionally ran a partial-correlation probe (train, n=85 after an alignment-limited join against a genuinely exogenous prior-day-total proxy): raw r(morning,remainder)=+0.145 collapses to partial r=-0.021 once the previous day's total post count is partialed out, while corr(morning, prev-day-total)=+0.64 and corr(remainder, prev-day-total)=+0.25. This is consistent with the report's own "day-level heterogeneity" explanation -- the effect looks like it rides on a shared "some days are just busier" latent factor rather than a morning-specific mechanism. Caveat: this probe's date-join dropped roughly 40 percent of rows to NaN in a same-session quick script, not part of the audited deliverable, so treat as corroborating, not dispositive -- but it points the same direction as the report's own honest caveat, not against it.
3. Sweep-then-freeze-then-evaluate-once, confirmed by code read AND by re-running. s4_formal_burst_rule computes p75/lift for every X candidate using only train_days inside the sweep loop; X_frozen/p75_frozen are then applied unchanged to both spans. Re-running the function directly reproduced X_frozen=3.0, p75_frozen=10.0 and both rows exactly. No sealed-visibility leak in the threshold selection.
4. Formal vs. literal measure different things, and the report says so. The formal test explicitly warns its own window "mostly just re-measures early activity correlates with total activity," and the literal test is explicitly labelled "descriptive only, not part of the formal PRIMARY/SECONDARY/TERTIARY gate." Neither is quoted as confirming the other's formal validity.
5. Robustness the study itself never ran, which the auditor ran instead. jackknife_drop_best is called for R1, R4, and every Q2 skill-vs-baseline pair, but never for S3 or S4 (grep-confirmed: jackknife appears only at lines 274, 475, 1072, 1259-1266, 1280 -- none inside s4_formal_burst_rule or s4_literal_calendar). This violates the prereg's unconditional "Single-outlier jackknife on every headline." Computed here: dropping the single most rule-favorable sealed day from the 13-point formal skill_vec moves the mean from -0.4014 to -0.4655 (sign holds, effect strengthens). Leave-one-out on the 211-point train correlation moves r between +0.205 and +0.266, never flipping sign. Conclusion: the missing check does not change the verdict, but its absence from the deliverable is a real, confirmable gap against the prereg's own mandate, on exactly the number that most needed it.

Bottom line on the positive finding: it survives every adversarial probe run against it (exact reproduction, train-only sweep confirmed twice, mixed-sign non-degenerate CI, auditor-computed jackknife holds and strengthens, partial correlation supports rather than undercuts the report's own "day-level heterogeneity, not exhaustion" framing). The one real defect is procedural (missing jackknife / undersized sealed corroboration slice), not a wrong number.

## Findings (most severe first)

[Class A] [MEDIUM] Confirmed DST-anchoring bug in DailySeries (regime_discovery_2026-07-29.py:533-545) silently drifts Elon daily windows off noon-ET for roughly 4 months of TRAIN.
Evidence: a dynamic test iterating d += 86400 from ELON_CLEAN_START (2025-09-01 12:00 ET) shows the ET wall-clock anchor silently shifts to 11:00 ET starting day-index 62 (2025-11-02, the "fall back" DST transition) and does not self-correct to noon until day-index 188 (2026-03-08, "spring forward") -- 126 consecutive days anchored 1 hour early. Both transition dates fall entirely inside TRAIN (WALL_ELON = 2026-04-01); the entire SEALED span (Apr 1 - Jun 27, 2026) is unaffected and correctly noon-anchored, since no further DST transition occurs before the study's end date.
Why it's wrong: every TRAIN-side use of daily.days (tercile-cut computation, S3/S4 threshold-and-percentile freezing, B1-B4/S2 hyperparameter selection via select_hmm_states/select_ridge_alpha) ran on windows that were actually "11am-to-11am" instead of "noon-to-noon" for roughly 60 percent of TRAIN days. The code comment at line 534 calls this pattern "already audited-clean" -- that claim is false; the byte-identical bug exists in the referenced source, pattern_discovery_2026-07-26.py::DailySeries (lines 96-104), meaning this is an inherited, previously-unflagged defect now propagated into a second study.
Contained blast radius: does NOT touch SEALED scoring (all reported skill/CI/lift/logloss headlines are computed on the correctly-anchored SEALED span), so no reported number here is confirmed wrong. It does mean the S1/S3/S4 threshold-freezing and B1-B4/S2 hyperparameter selection ran on a partially mis-anchored TRAIN sample.
Fix: rebuild DailySeries to re-derive each day's boundary via a fresh pd.Timestamp(calendar_date, tz=ET) + pd.Timedelta(hours=12) localization per day (the pattern s4_literal_calendar already uses correctly), not by accumulating raw 86400-second offsets from a single anchor. Re-run and confirm SEALED headlines are unchanged (expected) and spot-check whether any TRAIN-side threshold/hyperparameter selection shifts.

[Class C] [MEDIUM] Declared baseline B5 (MARKET) is completely unimplemented and its absence is undisclosed.
Evidence: a grep for B5, price_admissible, market_prob, xtracker, gamma, clob across the entire script returns zero matches. The prereg's Baselines section lists "B5 MARKET: implied bracket distribution where available ... every method scored against all of these," but neither Q1 (R1-R5) nor Q2 (B1-B4/S2) contains any market-price comparison. The summary's "What this does NOT show" section discloses two other deviations (Trump de-overlap, Q2 Elon-daily-only scope) but says nothing about B5.
Why it matters: per the project's own stated Pass-D standard, "Efficiency baseline = the MARKET price, not 50/50" is the one meaningful bar for a Polymarket-context study; without it, nothing here is checked against what the live market already knew. The canonical price layer was rebuilt the day before this run (2026-07-30 12:36, coverage now 0.0 percent missing on all targets) so it was directly available.
Fix: implement B5 against the fresh canonical price layer for the Elon 2-day auctions in Q1 (where live orderbooks exist), or add an explicit disclosed limitation line if it is deliberately out of scope for this pass.

[Class D] [MEDIUM] Mandatory "jackknife on every headline" was never run on S3/S4. See the recomputation and result under "THE ONE POSITIVE FINDING" above -- a compliance gap against the prereg, not a numeric error; verdict unchanged once supplied.

[Class D] [MEDIUM] Literal-calendar SEALED corroboration slice (n_big_morning=8) sits below SUCCESS_N_FLOOR=10 and carries no disqualifying sentinel, despite being cited by name in the bolded "Verdict (c): HOLDS IN REVERSE."
Evidence: q2_s4 has no exported per-row CSV; re-running s4_literal_calendar gives the 8 sealed big-morning days directly (dates 2026-04-01, 04-03, 04-12, 04-25, 05-22, 06-02, 06-12, 06-23). Leave-one-out on this 8-point set: the "median remainder given big morning" moves between 15.0 and 24.0 depending which day is dropped, and on 4 of 8 single-day drops it falls to exactly 15.0 -- tying the sealed "not-big"/overall baseline and erasing the apparent gap for that slice.
Why it matters: unlike R1/R4/R5, which all get an explicit "noise (n=X, below success floor)" sentinel or a [FLAGGED n<30] tag when small, this n=8 slice gets neither, and is one of only two pieces of evidence named in the bolded verdict sentence. The train-side literal evidence (n=211, r=+0.234, robust to full leave-one-out) is not in question; only this small sealed corroboration slice is fragile and under-labelled. Does not change the overall verdict, since the FORMAL test (sealed n_fired=13, floor-clearing, jackknife-robust) independently supports the same conclusion.

[Class A] [LOW-MEDIUM, informational] Undisclosed TRAIN/SEALED base-rate shift in Elon's underlying posting activity.
Evidence (from q2_gap_burst_features.csv, deduped per day): mean daily counting-posts falls from 44.2 (train, n=212) to 30.7 (sealed, n=87); the TRAIN-frozen tercile cuts classify 63.2 percent of SEALED days LOW (vs 35.8 percent of TRAIN) and only 8.0 percent HIGH (vs 33.5 percent of TRAIN).
This is legitimate WALL-respecting behavior (terciles are correctly frozen from train, never redefined on sealed, per the prereg), not a bug or leak. It is larger and more consequential than either of the two items the summary chose to disclose in "Data-integrity findings," and isn't mentioned. It's useful context: some of B2's poor sealed performance (static climatology anchored to a stale train-era rate) and the large base_rate_low=0.632 figure quoted inside the headline reverse-exhaustion finding are partly downstream of this shift, not purely a sign of method skill.

Summary text says "any Q1 finding at n<10 is emitted as an explicit noise sentinel" -- literally true per the code's strict less-than comparison, but doesn't cover trump_7day's sealed n=10 (exactly at, not below, the floor). No practical harm: both R1 and R4 correctly report "unproven (CI includes zero)" for trump regardless, and R1's own jackknife flags sign_holds=False -- the honesty is preserved in substance, the boundary prose is just slightly loose.
